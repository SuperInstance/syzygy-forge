# Reader-side evaluation — learned micro table vs fixed table, on real footage

*2026-10-11. The stage-5 gate asks two things of a learned table: a probe gain and a **reader** gain — models must actually read more truth out of the learned plane than the fixed one, at equal characters per frame. The forge v0 measured the proxy (quantisation error: fixed 151.18, learned micro 44.63). This experiment measures the thing itself.*

## Setup

- **Frames:** 40 frames spread deterministically across the docks clip (`np.linspace(0, 296, 40)` rounded; list in `frames_sampled.json`). One clip, one location, daylight harbour — same corpus the tables were trained on, frames **not** held out from table training (the micro table's T1 training used the 238-frame train split only; this is a readability comparison of two frozen planes, not a generalisation test of the table).
- **Two renders per frame, same grid (148×80 cells, 11,840 chars), same 10-step ramp ` .:-=+*#%@`:**
  - **fixed** — the kernel's fixed-table tone plane: the shipped `syzygy-video` docks-text/tone frames (one-line file header stripped; verified cell-for-cell against the forge corpus on all 40 frames, 0 mismatches — and the forge verified that corpus against the kernel on all 3,516,480 cells).
  - **learned** — the learned micro table (id `0x7ccb88b5`) rendered by the forge's own renderer (`src/eval.py: learned_plane`, copied verbatim; reproduction-checked against the forge's shipped compare panels for frames 10/120, exact match): biased-argmax assignment, codewords ordered by centroid mean_luma, rank mapped onto the same ramp. **The only difference between the two prompts is which codeword each cell gets.** The learned plane changes 7,215–10,098 of 11,840 cells per frame (61–85%) vs fixed.
- **Questions (5 per frame, frozen wording in `run_readers.py`):** Q1 brightest quadrant, Q2 darkest quadrant, Q3 tone-centroid side (left/right of centre), Q4 edge-mass side, Q5 vertical band with most edge activity. **Every answer computed programmatically from the source pixels (the NV12 Y plane) — never from a model** — with the full computation recorded per frame in `questions.jsonl`. Definitions: quadrant = Y sums over pixel halves; centroid = luma-weighted x̄ (left iff x̄ < 148 px); edges = Sobel |gx|+|gy| > 200 on Y, counted per half (Q4) and per row-third band (Q5). Worked example, frame 23: quadrant sums TL 2,347,524 / TR 3,090,015 / BL 2,985,056 / BR 2,991,720 → Q1 TR, Q2 TL; centroid x̄ = 77.08 cells → Q3 right; edge counts left 2,529 / right 3,363 → Q4 right; band counts top 4,748 / middle 1,144 / bottom **0** → Q5 top.
- **Readers:** MiniMax-Text-01 (stock CLI) and GLM glm-4.5-flash via the reading experiment's `glm_call.py` (thinking disabled — same documented deviation as stage 1). One batched call per (reader, condition, frame); the prompt (`GUIDE.md`: the v2 procedures guide adapted to region questions — tally by 10-row strips, per region, Work lines before answer lines) never names the condition. Third condition: **blind** (same questions, no frame). Temperature 0.7 (both callers' defaults), so answers are samples, not deterministic reads — as in stage 1.
- **Scoring:** frozen-style exact match after per-question normalization, pure functions in `run_readers.py`; unparsed = wrong; first successful record wins. **Coverage 240/240** successful records (40 frames × 3 conditions × 2 readers); 1 failed attempt retained (GLM 429 on learned/frame 182, backfilled on re-run).

## Findings

1. **For MiniMax, the learned plane is more readable on every question type.** Gains are uniform, not carried by one question: Q1 +10, Q2 +5, Q3 +15, Q4 +10, Q5 +17.5 points. Its fixed-plane lift over blind is essentially zero (+0.005) — on the fixed plane MiniMax reads no more region truth than it guesses — while the learned plane lifts +0.120. On the **clear-margin subset** (quadrant margins ≥ 2% of total, centroid ≥ 2 cells, band margin ≥ 10%) the gap widens: fixed 0.310 → learned **0.476**.
2. **For GLM, a wash overall — built from violent disagreements.** Learned wins edge-mass decisively (Q4 0.350 → 0.525; learned-only wins 10 frames vs fixed-only 3) and Q5 modestly (+5), but its centroid reading *collapses* on the learned plane (Q3 0.600 → 0.325; fixed-only wins 14 frames vs learned-only 3) and Q2 drops 7.5 points. On clear-margin frames GLM's learned also edges ahead (0.365 → 0.397); the headline −0.025 is substantially a near-tie effect. The two versions produce genuinely different readings, not noise around one reading: the readers give the *same* answer to both versions only 28–55% of the time per question.
3. **The sharpest single failure difference — MiniMax, frame 23, Q5.** Ground truth could hardly be clearer: edge counts top 4,748 / middle 1,144 / bottom **0** (the bottom band contains not one strong edge). On the **fixed** plane MiniMax produced neat, confident, wholly invented tallies — *"Top band edge count: 78. Middle band edge count: 90. Bottom band edge count: 120. The bottom band has the most edge activity."* — and answered **bottom**, the one band with literally nothing in it. On the **learned** plane, same frame, it answered **top**. The pattern repeats on the other two widest-margin band frames (296: fixed → bottom, true bottom count 0, learned → top ✓; 205, the mirror: fixed → top, true top count 22 vs bottom 7,900, learned → bottom ✓). On the three clearest edge-band frames in the set, the fixed plane went 0/3 — each time naming an empty or near-empty band — and the learned plane 3/3. A plausible mechanism, labelled as interpretation: the fixed tone char tracks absolute luma, so bright-but-flat regions (glare, water, sky) fill with dense high characters that read as "busy"; the learned assignment is driven by local-contrast features (its 8 features include directional means and texture), so its character *changes* concentrate where structure actually is, and the guide's "count the sharp steps" instruction lands closer to true Sobel mass.
4. **The counterweight — GLM, frame 83, Q1.** Truth TR by a wide margin (TR 3,438,422 vs runner-up BL 2,941,908). On the fixed plane GLM's (invented, but correctly-shaped) tallies put TR on top and it answered TR. On the learned plane its strip tallies claimed left ≫ right in every strip (1621 vs 688, 2023 vs 915, …) and it answered BL. The learned plane can mislead a reader's impression as well as inform it; finding 2's Q3 collapse is this failure at scale.
5. **The absolute ceiling stands over both planes.** A per-question constant answerer scores a 0.476 composite on this truth set — **above every reader × version cell** (best: MiniMax learned, 0.430). The Work lines show why: at 148×80 neither reader actually executes the strip tallies — the numbers are confabulated in the shape of the procedure (finding 3's 78/90/120; GLM repeating "Left Half: 740, Right Half: 740" strip after strip on frame 23 learned). What is being measured at this grid size is therefore which plane's *statistics* survive impressionistic reading — not verified sums. This is the Typesafe lane's design claim confirmed from the reader side: at full grid, region truth needs the digest (precomputed sums) as the channel; raw-grid reading tops out where these numbers sit. GLM's Q1 is the extreme case: **0.150 in both versions — below its own blind score (0.300) and below chance (0.25)**; on this footage its brightest-quadrant impression is anti-correlated with the truth whichever plane it reads.
6. **Format is a confound, and it is version-correlated for GLM.** Unparsed slots (of 200): MiniMax fixed 13, learned 0, blind 17; GLM fixed 25, learned 6, blind 60 (the v1 template-echo failure, back in force without a frame). Parsed-only accuracies: MiniMax 0.337 / **0.430** / 0.339 — its learned advantage and its learned-only lift (+0.09) survive the adjustment. GLM parsed-only: fixed 0.400, learned 0.335, blind 0.400 — parsed-only, GLM's learned cell is its *worst*, and its fixed and blind cells tie. The strict table is the frozen headline; the honest GLM statement is "no readable-truth gain, under either scoring."

## Caveats (stated, not buried)

- **The unit under test is table + the forge's renderer.** Its rank-uniform mapping (16 codewords squeezed onto 10 ramp steps by centroid-luma rank) preserves brightness *order* but not *intervals* — and Q1/Q3 are interval questions (mass ratios, balance points). GLM's centroid collapse may be partly a property of that mapping rather than of the table. A luma-calibrated mapping is the obvious next variable; it was deliberately not introduced here, because the task fixed the renderer as the forge's.
- **Definition sensitivity in the truth itself:** the scored Q4/Q5 truth is the Sobel-*count* argmax; the *mass* argmax disagrees on 3/40 frames (Q4) and 2/40 (Q5) — flagged per frame in `questions.jsonl`. On those frames "truth" depends on which edge measure a reader could in principle perceive.
- **One clip, one harbour, daylight**; readers at temperature 0.7; the learned table trained on this same clip (see Setup). Nothing here estimates cross-scene generalisation — that is what a bigger corpus (forge README, item 6) is for.

## Verdict

**Split — and the split is the result.** The learned plane demonstrably carries more *readable* truth than the fixed one for MiniMax: better on all five question types, +11.5 points overall, the only reader×version cell with a double-digit lift, growing to +16.6 points where the truth is clear. For GLM it carries no more on balance: an edge-reading gain cancelled by a centroid collapse. Against the stage-5 gate's reader bar (learned ≥ fixed + 5 points): **met by one reader, missed by the other — the gate is not cleanly passed.** The direction the proxy promised (quantisation error 151 → 45) is real but reader-dependent, and both readers remain under the constant-answer ceiling that raw 148×80 grids impose. Two consequences, specific rather than generic: (1) the next learned-table comparison should vary the *render mapping* alongside the table — the interval distortion is now a named suspect with a frame number on it (GLM, 83); (2) reader-side claims at full grid should route region questions through the digest, reserving raw-plane reading for what it measurably does — position and structure impressions, where the learned plane's advantage, when a reader can take it, is largest (Q5 +17.5, Q3 +15 for MiniMax).
